Goals a player scores, credited to the last player on the scoring team to touch the puck. It's the stat every other number on this site is trying to predict.

Credit for the last one or two players on the scoring team to touch the puck before the goal scorer. The first is the primary assist, the second the secondary.

Goals plus assists, each worth one point. It's the oldest way to rank skaters, and it counts a secondary assist the same as a goal.

Goals for minus goals against while a skater is on the ice at even strength or shorthanded. Power-play goals don't count, and it can't tell who caused the goal.

Total minutes a player is penalized, at two for a minor, five for a major and 10 for a misconduct. Penalties he draws don't offset it.

Goals (PPG) and points (PPP) scored while a player's team has more skaters on the ice than its opponent. They show who a coach trusts with the man advantage.

Goals (SHG) and points (SHP) scored while a player's team has fewer skaters on the ice than its opponent, usually on a penalty kill.

Goals (EVG) and points (EVP) scored with the same number of skaters on each side, including 4-on-4 and 3-on-3. Most of a season's scoring happens here.

The goal that put the winner one ahead of the loser's final total, so the third goal of a 5-2 win. It can come in the first period.

Goals scored in overtime, which end the game on the spot. Regular-season overtime is five minutes of 3-on-3; playoff overtime is full 20-minute periods at five-on-five.

Goals plus the shots a goalie stopped. Misses, posts and blocked shots don't count, so it's the narrowest of the shot stats.

Goals divided by shots on goal. A shooter who runs far above his career rate in one season usually drifts back toward it the next.

How many minutes a player is on the ice, in total or per game (ATOI). It's the clearest read on how much a coach trusts someone.

Faceoffs won divided by faceoffs taken (FOW counts the wins). 50% is break-even, and the best regular centers win around 60%.

Body checks credited by each arena's off-ice scorers, who don't all count them the same way. A team that's hitting a lot usually doesn't have the puck.

Shot attempts a player stops with his body before they reach the net. Blocks prevent goals, but a team that blocks a lot is usually defending a lot.

Takeaways (TK) are pucks a player steals; giveaways (GV) are pucks he loses on his own. Arena scorers log both and vary by rink, so treat them as rough counts.

The number of games a player appeared in. Per-game stats divide by it, and small totals are where the noise lives.

Points divided by games played. It puts a player who missed time on the same footing as one who played all 82, and a point a game marks a star.

Penalties a player draws from opponents, each one a power play for his team. Drawn minus taken is penalty differential, a small but real piece of a player's value.

Saves divided by shots on goal faced. It treats a point shot and a backdoor tap-in the same, which is why expected-goals stats grade goalies more fairly.

Goals a goalie allows per 60 minutes. It ignores how many shots he faced and how dangerous they were, so it grades his defense along with him.

A game in which a goalie plays the whole way and allows no goals. If two goalies combine for a clean sheet, neither gets credit.

A goalie's wins, regulation losses and overtime or shootout losses (OTL). The decision goes to the goalie in net for the deciding goal, so it tracks his team too.

Expected goals a goalie faced minus the goals he allowed. Positive means he stopped more than an average goalie would have on the same shots.

Goals a goalie saved compared with a league-average save percentage on the same number of shots. Unlike GSAx, it treats every shot as equally dangerous.

A start where a goalie beats the league-average save percentage, or posts .885 on 20 shots or fewer. A really bad start (RBS) is anything under .850.

Save percentage on high-danger shots only, the attempts from right in front of the net. It isolates the saves that swing games, though the samples get small fast.

The save percentage an average goalie would post on the shots a goalie faced, built from expected goals. Subtract it from his real save percentage to see what he added.

Shots against (SA) are shots on goal a goalie faced, goals against (GA) the ones that went in, saves (SV) the rest. Save percentage is SV divided by SA.

Standings points earned divided by points available, two per game. It's how you compare teams that have played different numbers of games.

Wins in regulation (RW), or in regulation and overtime (ROW), leaving out shootout wins. RW is one of the NHL's first standings tiebreakers.

Goals for minus goals against. Over a full season it tracks standings points closely, which makes it a quick check on whether a record is real.

Power-play goals divided by power-play opportunities. It says how well a team converts and nothing about how many power plays it draws.

The share of opponents' power plays a team kills off without allowing a goal. It's the mirror image of power-play percentage.

The probability that an unblocked shot becomes a goal, priced against hundreds of thousands of past NHL shots like it. Add them up for a player's or team's total.

Expected goals on the shots a player takes himself. It measures how many good looks he gets, whether or not they go in.

Goals minus expected goals, for a player or team. Positive means the finishing beat an average shooter on the same chances, and a single season carries a lot of luck.

Every shot attempt, on goal, missed or blocked, counted for and against while a player or team is on the ice. CF% is the share; the NHL calls it SAT.

Corsi without the blocked shots, so unblocked attempts for and against. It's the same set of shots expected goals uses, and the NHL calls it USAT.

A player's Corsi share with him on the ice minus his team's share without him. It shows whether he drives play better or worse than the rest of the roster.

On-ice shooting percentage plus on-ice save percentage, usually at five-on-five. Everyone drifts back toward 1.000 (or 100), so a number far from it usually means luck.

Shot attempts Natural Stat Trick rates as most dangerous, mostly from right in front of the net, with extra weight for rebounds and rush shots. HDCF% is a team's share.

Shot attempts Natural Stat Trick counts as scoring chances, based on where they came from, with extra credit for rebounds and rush shots. SCF% is a team's share.

A team's share of the shots on goal in its games, or in a player's minutes. It's the narrowest shot-share stat, since misses and blocks don't count.

A team's share of the goals scored in its games, or in a player's minutes, usually at five-on-five. It's the result that xGF% and CF% try to predict.

The shooting (oiSH%) and save (oiSV%) percentages of a player's team while he's on the ice. Both swing with luck, and their sum is PDO.

The share of a player's shifts that begin with an offensive-zone (OZS%) or defensive-zone (DZS%) faceoff. Coaches use them to shelter or bury players, and they tilt results.

Measures of how strong a player's opponents (QoC) and linemates (QoT) were, usually weighted by shared ice time. They put a player's results in context.

One number for a player's total value over a replacement-level player, in goals (GAR) or wins (WAR). Evolving-Hockey's version also comes in an expected-goals form (xGAR).

Regularized adjusted plus-minus, a regression that estimates each player's impact while controlling for teammates, opponents, zone starts and score. It's a building block of public WAR models.

Any stat divided by ice time and scaled to 60 minutes. It levels the field between a top-line forward and a fourth-liner who plays half as much.

The share of goals scored while a player is on the ice that he scored or assisted on. It shows who's driving the offense and who's along for the ride.

A single-game rating that rolls goals, assists, shots, blocks, penalties, faceoffs and on-ice shot and goal results into one number. This site averages MoneyPuck's and HockeyStatCards' versions.

How a team carries the puck into the offensive zone, with control or by dumping it in, and how it gets out of its own. The NHL doesn't log either.

Every shot attempt a player takes himself, on goal, missed or blocked. It's a pure measure of shooting volume.

Stats reweighted for the score, because trailing teams push and leading teams sit back. Unadjusted, a team that leads a lot looks worse at controlling play than it is.

How dangerous a team's or player's shots are on average, usually measured as expected goals per unblocked attempt. Location matters most, then shot type and what happened just before.

This site's 0-to-100 rating of a player's value this season, with 50 as the NHL average. Skaters are scored on goals above replacement, goalies on goals saved above expected.

Where a number ranks in its comparison group on a 0-to-100 scale, so the 90th percentile beats 90% of the group. Forwards and defensemen are usually ranked separately here.

Both teams with five skaters and a goalie on the ice, which is most of every game. It's the default for comparisons because special-teams time varies so much.

A power play (5v4) is time with an extra skater after an opponent's penalty; the penalty kill (4v5) is the other side. Both are rated here per 60 minutes.

Every game state combined, five-on-five, power play, penalty kill, 4-on-4, 3-on-3 and empty net. Individual totals like ixG use it unless a column says otherwise.

The standings rebuilt on expected goals. Each team's all-situations xGF and xGA become a Pythagorean win rate, then points, and all 32 teams are ranked the same way.

MoneyPuck's simulation of the rest of the season, giving projected points and playoff odds. This site freezes the opening-night projection and shows the updated one, re-run after every result.
